About

Systems, end to end.

I build software the way I solve problems: work it out from first principles, get it correct, then keep going until it is something a person can actually use.

Data structures and algorithms are how I think. 600+ problems across LeetCode, GeeksforGeeks and CodeChef, most of them on ordinary days when nothing was due — and it shows up in the work rather than just on a profile. It is why my graph engine stores a million edges in compressed rows instead of a naive adjacency list, why a booking assistant takes a Redis lock before two people can claim the same slot, and why I check a model against ground truth instead of trusting a score that looks good.

Right now I'm a Software & AI Engineer Intern at NimbleBiz.ai, where I built the filtering and table engine that 1,000+ users work in every day, and shipped production modules for voice and chat AI bots. Before that I worked on payments and data pipelines at YesCity. What I care about in both is the last ten per cent — the retry when a page fails, the honest log of what went wrong, the empty state nobody thinks about. Accurate and usable, in whatever domain the work happens to be.

I learn something new most days, and I have never grown out of finding it interesting. When I get stuck I ask — seniors at work, faculty and batchmates at college — because a question asked early costs an hour and a wrong assumption costs a week. I try to be on the other side of that too: I'm a Senior Advisor on the technical core team of Knuth Programming Hub, one of JIIT's most respected clubs, where I help juniors get unstuck, review how they're approaching a problem, and run practice sessions.

Next up: going deeper into system design and cloud, so the things I build hold up when a lot more people show up. In my free time I play chess on chess.com — the same habit as the rest of it, really: look a few moves ahead, weigh the trade-off, commit to the line.
